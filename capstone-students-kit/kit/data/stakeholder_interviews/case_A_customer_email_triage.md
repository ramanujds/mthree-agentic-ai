# Interview transcript - Case A: Inbound customer e-mail triage
Interviewee: Aisha Bello, Head of Customer Support, Meridian Bank. Interviewer: you. (Synthetic case.)

**You:** Walk me through what happens when a customer e-mail arrives.
**Aisha:** About 2,000 a week land in one shared inbox. Two people read them in the morning and sort them into queues: card problems, payments, account changes, complaints, and "other". Then the queue owners pick them up.
**You:** How long does the sorting take?
**Aisha:** It varies. Some days the inbox is a day behind. Customers with an urgent fraud problem sometimes wait until the afternoon before anybody sees it. That's what keeps me awake.
**You:** What would success look like?
**Aisha:** I want it fully automatic. Nothing should wait for a human. And it must be 99% accurate, otherwise my team won't trust it.
**You:** What happens today when an e-mail is sorted wrongly?
**Aisha:** The queue owner moves it. Nobody tracks how often. I'd guess 1 in 10, but honestly I don't know.
**You:** Are there e-mails that must never be handled automatically?
**Aisha:** Complaints go to a named person, always. And anything that mentions fraud must reach Compliance within 24 hours - that's policy. Oh, and customers attach photos of IDs and statements quite often.
**You:** Should the system reply to customers?
**Aisha:** Eventually yes, an instant acknowledgement would be lovely. But legal said no automatic replies that give financial advice. We never agreed what counts as advice.
**You:** Which systems hold the customer's data?
**Aisha:** The core banking system and the CRM. IT said API access is "possible" but I have never seen it.
**You:** Any constraints on where data can go?
**Aisha:** Nothing leaves the bank's network. Beyond that, ask Security.
