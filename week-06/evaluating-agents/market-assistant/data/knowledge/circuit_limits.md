# Circuit Limits and Price Bands

## What is a circuit limit
A circuit limit (price band) is the maximum percentage by which a share price may rise or fall from the previous day's closing price in a single trading day. Exchanges set it to curb extreme volatility. The upper circuit is the highest allowed price for the day and the lower circuit is the lowest allowed price for the day.

## Band percentages
Price bands are 2%, 5%, 10% or 20%. The band of each stock is set by the exchange and can change. Large, liquid stocks in this sandbox have a 20% band, except where the quote shows otherwise. The quote tool returns the band percentage, the upper circuit price and the lower circuit price for each stock.

## What happens at the circuit
When a stock touches its upper or lower circuit, no trade can happen beyond that price. If a stock hits the circuit, trading in it may be halted for the rest of the day. Orders priced outside the band are rejected.

## Index-wide circuit breakers
If the Nifty 50 or the Sensex moves by 10%, 15% or 20% in a day, trading in the entire market is halted for a cooling-off period.
